# Learning work samples · Agueda Schwartz

I'm a learning designer. These are the working documents from one program I designed in 2026: helping the staff of a research and development lab use Claude, an AI assistant, in their everyday work. People's names and internal project names are removed.

**5** colleagues in a pilot of about two and a half weeks · **about 30** staff in the workshop · **6** teams · **5** prototype concepts

## The program

The lab used Claude, and a staff survey showed most people were already using AI on their own. The gap wasn't access. People wanted to see how their colleagues used it, and to practice on their real work.

So I designed a two-hour workshop around people's own tasks, and before running it I tested the workflow with five colleagues on their real work for about two and a half weeks. What they ran into changed the design: teach people to refine their prompts, be honest that Claude misses things, and show when doing it by hand is faster. I also previewed the approach at a staff AI forum session and collected the tasks people most wanted help with.

A week before the workshop, a co-facilitator proposed turning it into a team competition on one shared project. I rebuilt the facilitator track around that and kept the pilot's lessons in the challenges and the debriefs. About 30 staff in six teams took part, and the teams presented five prototype concepts. Since then I've supported the lab's AI forum, where staff keep learning from each other.

## Design decisions

- **Pilot before the workshop.** Five colleagues tried the workflow on their own work first, and their feedback shaped the design: teach prompt iteration, set honest expectations on accuracy, and name where Claude falls short.
- **Real work, learned by doing.** Teams worked on a real past lab project, the track had no demos or lectures, and the facilitators coached instead of presenting.
- **Iteration is the skill.** Prompts were starting points, not scripts.
- **Keep the lessons when the format changes.** When a co-facilitator proposed a team competition a week before, I rebuilt the facilitator track around it and kept the pilot's lessons in the challenges and the debriefs.

## Supporting materials

7. [Challenge dashboard](07-challenge-dashboard.html): a one-page app I proposed for the workshop. Teams register, check off challenges as they finish them, and a facilitator enters scores and reveals the winner. I designed it and built it with Claude; the team decided to run the workshop without it. Open it in a browser; progress is saved only in that browser.
