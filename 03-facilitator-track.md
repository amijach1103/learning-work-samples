# The AI Workflow Challenge · Facilitator Track

**Work sample, Agueda Schwartz.** A two-hour, hands-on team competition for about 30 staff at a research and development lab, February 2026. I wrote this facilitator track; the shared challenge framework was a co-facilitator's, and I rebuilt the track around it. Internal project and strategy names are replaced and co-facilitators are anonymized.

**Context.** A research and development lab had chosen Claude as its AI tool, and the Ops team asked me to lead a workshop to help staff use it in their real work. A staff survey showed most people already used AI; what they wanted was to see how peers used it and to practice hands-on. I piloted a workflow with five colleagues on their real work, and their feedback shaped the design: teach prompt iteration, set honest expectations on accuracy, and name where Claude falls short. A week before the workshop, a co-facilitator proposed a team competition on one shared project. I adopted it and wrote this track around it, keeping the pilot's lessons in the challenges and the debriefs.

**What happened.** About 30 staff in six teams took part at an in-person onsite, and the teams presented five prototype concepts. My flight was canceled, so my co-facilitators ran the room while I coached the four people who joined virtually as their own team.

- **Length:** 2 hours 15 minutes, with a 15-minute break
- **Facilitators:** Agueda, Co-facilitator A, Co-facilitator B
- **Format:** Team competition (about 4-5 teams of 3-4 people)
- **Core activity:** Reimagining a past lab initiative ("the Project") using Claude with Google Drive and Asana

---

## Overview

This is a game. Every minute supports the competition. No disconnected activities, no demos, no lectures. Participants learn by doing. Facilitators are coaches, not presenters.

**Design principles:**
- Prompts are starting points, not scripts: iteration is the skill
- Scoring is honor system plus screenshots
- Sprint 1 = Google Drive, Sprint 2 = Asana (clean split)
- The lab's new strategic direction is the mystery ingredient, revealed Iron Chef style before Sprint 2
- Capstone is within Sprint 2 time (fast teams get to it)
- Sticky-note data collected through the capstone slide instead of a separate activity

## Detailed Facilitator Track

### 0:00–0:08 | THE CHALLENGE BEGINS (8 min)

**Lead:** Co-facilitator B opens, then Agueda and Co-facilitator A

**SLIDE:** Title slide, "The AI Workflow Challenge"

**Co-facilitator B says:**
> "Welcome to the AI Workflow Challenge. This is not a training. This is a competition. For the next two hours, your teams will use Claude AI, with Google Drive and Asana integrations, to tackle a real lab project. Points for each challenge. Winning team gets bragging rights."

**Agueda says:**
> "The project you're working with today is a real lab initiative. Everything you need is in Google Drive and Asana. Claude is your coworker. Your job is to use it well."

**Co-facilitator A says:**
> "You'll get a challenge sheet with 11 challenges across two sprints. Each challenge has a point value. Some are easy. Some will push you. Let's form teams."

**SLIDE:** "The Challenge"

**Action: form teams**
- Count off or pre-assign about 4-5 teams of 3-4 people
- Each team picks a team name (30 seconds, keep it fast)
- Hand out challenge sheets (one per person)
- Each team designates a "scorekeeper" to track points

**Facilitator behavior:**
- Keep energy HIGH. This is the opening. Set the tone.
- If someone isn't set up with Claude, pair them with someone who is. Don't troubleshoot now.

### 0:08–0:10 | RULES OF THE KITCHEN (2 min)

**Lead:** Co-facilitator B

**SLIDE:** "Rules of the Kitchen"

**Co-facilitator B says:**
> "Quick rules. Screenshot or screen-record your results as proof. It's the honor system, but we'll want to see highlights. Your challenge handout is your guide. Most importantly: ask Claude first, not us. We're coaches, not answer keys. If your setup isn't working, pair with someone whose is. Ready? Sprint 1 starts now."

### 0:10–0:48 | SPRINT 1: GOOGLE DRIVE CHALLENGES #1-6 (38 min)

**Lead:** All facilitators circulate

**SLIDE:** "Sprint 1: Google Drive" (challenge overview with points), then an ambient timer slide

**Challenges in play:**

| # | Challenge | Points |
|---|-----------|--------|
| 1 | Find the Project's docs in Google Drive using Claude | 2 |
| 2 | Get Claude to summarize a specific Project document | 3 |
| 3 | Ask Claude to extract key milestones or deliverables | 3 |
| 4 | Compare two Project documents (find differences, evolution) | 5 |
| 5 | Ask Claude to identify gaps or missing info in the Project docs | 5 |
| 6 | Get Claude to synthesize across multiple docs into a briefing | 7 |

**Total Sprint 1 points: 25**

**Facilitator behavior during Sprint 1**

DO:
- Walk the room continuously. Visit every team at least twice.
- Coach prompt iteration: "What did Claude give you? How could you refine that prompt?"
- Push teams toward harder challenges: "You nailed #1 and #2. Try #4, that's where the real points are."
- Notice and amplify good prompting: "Team 3 just did something smart: they gave Claude specific context before asking."
- Keep track of team energy. If a team is stuck, give a nudge (not the answer).

DON'T:
- Write prompts for them.
- Troubleshoot integrations for more than 2 minutes per team (pair them instead).
- Let any team sit idle. Redirect them to a different challenge if they're stuck.

**5-minute warning at 0:43. Any facilitator announces:**
> "Five minutes left in Sprint 1. Finish what you're working on. Make sure your screenshots are saved."

### 0:48–0:55 | JUDGES' TABLE #1 (7 min)

**Lead:** Co-facilitator A moderates

**SLIDE:** "Judges' Table #1"

**Co-facilitator A says:**
> "Time's up for Sprint 1. Let's hear from each team. You've got 60 seconds: What's your best find so far? Where did Claude fail you?"

**Format:**
- Go team by team, about 60 seconds each
- Co-facilitator A keeps it moving: "Great, next team"
- After all teams share, facilitators add 1-2 sharp observations

**Facilitator observations to surface (pick 1-2):**
- "I noticed several teams had to refine their prompts 2-3 times to get useful results. That's the skill."
- "Team X tried something interesting: they gave Claude context about WHY they needed the info, not just WHAT."
- "Claude struggled with [specific thing]. Good to know its limits."

**This IS the reflection.** Framed as competition commentary, not a "learning moment."

### 0:55–1:00 | SCORE CHECK (5 min)

**Lead:** Co-facilitator B

**SLIDE:** Scoreboard (editable)

**Co-facilitator B says:**
> "Let's see where we stand. Scorekeepers, how many points does your team have?"

**Action:**
- Go team by team. Co-facilitator B writes scores on the slide (or whiteboard).
- Read out standings: "In first place with X points..."
- Build energy: "Sprint 2 has 22 more points up for grabs, and a mystery ingredient."

### 1:00–1:15 | BREAK (15 min)

**Facilitator behavior during break:**
- Help anyone with setup issues (this is troubleshooting time).
- Check in with struggling teams quietly.
- Agueda, Co-facilitator A, Co-facilitator B: quick huddle to calibrate. How are teams doing? Any adjustments needed for Sprint 2?
- Make sure the reveal slide is ready.

### 1:15–1:20 | MYSTERY INGREDIENT REVEAL (5 min)

**Lead:** Co-facilitator A or Agueda

**SLIDE:** Mystery ingredient reveal, the lab's new strategic direction (dramatic)

**Co-facilitator A or Agueda says:**
> "In Sprint 1, you dug into what the Project was. You explored the documents. You found the history. But a revival needs a direction."

*Pause. Build the moment.*

> "In Iron Chef, the mystery ingredient changes everything. Here's yours."

*Reveal slide.*

> "[The lab's new strategic direction, in one line.] Your Project 2.0 in Asana needs to reflect this. How would you redesign the Project to serve it?"

> "You don't need to be an expert in it. Ask Claude. Give it the context from Sprint 1. Let it help you think through the connection. Sprint 2 starts now."

**SLIDE:** "Sprint 2: Asana + Capstone"

### 1:20–1:52 | SPRINT 2: ASANA CHALLENGES #7-10 + CAPSTONE #11 (32 min)

**Lead:** All facilitators circulate

**SLIDE:** Sprint 2 challenge overview, then an ambient timer slide

**Challenges in play:**

| # | Challenge | Points |
|---|-----------|--------|
| 7 | Create a Project 2.0 in Asana using Claude | 3 |
| 8 | Add sections and tasks framed around the new direction | 4 |
| 9 | Get Claude to write a project status update | 3 |
| 10 | Have Claude organize tasks by priority or timeline | 5 |
| 11 | **Capstone:** Create a takeaway slide: your Project 2.0 vision + one tedious task from your real work Claude could help with | 7 |

**Total Sprint 2 points: 22**
**Grand total possible: 47 points**

**Facilitator behavior during Sprint 2**

DO:
- Push teams to connect Sprint 1 findings to Sprint 2 work: "What did you learn about the Project from the docs? How does that shape your Asana project?"
- Coach the connection to the new direction: "Where is it today? Where does it need to get to? How does the Project bridge that?"
- Remind teams about the capstone: "Challenge #11 is worth 7 points. Don't skip it."
- Help teams think about the capstone's real-work element: "What's something tedious you do every week that Claude might help with?"

DON'T:
- Explain the new direction in depth. Let Claude help them figure it out.
- Let teams skip the capstone. It's the highest-value challenge AND how we collect sticky-note data.

**5-minute warning at 1:47. Any facilitator announces:**
> "Five minutes. If you haven't started your capstone slide (#11), start it now. It's worth 7 points."

### 1:52–1:58 | JUDGES' TABLE #2 + GALLERY WALK (6 min)

**Lead:** All facilitators

**SLIDE:** "Judges' Table #2 + Gallery Walk"

**Co-facilitator A says:**
> "Sprint 2 is done. Let's see your capstone slides. Each team: pull up your slide. We're doing a quick gallery walk."

**Format:**
- Teams display their capstone slides (on their screens or projected).
- Quick walk-through: each team gets 30 seconds to pitch their Project 2.0 vision.
- Facilitators and other teams can react, but keep it fast.

**Facilitator behavior:**
- Look for: creative connections to the new direction, strong use of Claude, real-work pain points mentioned.
- Note the "tedious tasks" mentioned. These replace sticky notes for planning next month's AI Forum.

### 1:58–2:05 | FINAL SCORING + WINNER (7 min)

**Lead:** Co-facilitator B

**SLIDE:** Final scoreboard

**Co-facilitator B says:**
> "Let's tally it up. Scorekeepers, give me your Sprint 2 points."

**Action:**
- Add Sprint 2 scores to the board.
- Calculate totals.
- Build suspense: "And the winner of the AI Workflow Challenge is..."
- Announce the winner. Celebrate. Applause.

**Co-facilitator B says:**
> "Well done, everyone. You just used Claude to do real work, finding information, synthesizing documents, building project plans, in about two hours."

### 2:05–2:10 | YOUR TURN (5 min)

**Lead:** Agueda

**SLIDE:** "Your Turn"

**Agueda says:**
> "You just used Claude to explore a project, synthesize documents, and build an Asana plan. That's real work. Now think about YOUR actual job."

> "What's ONE workflow from your real work you'll try with Claude next week? Not someday, next week. Write it down."

*Give 60 seconds to write.*

> "Now turn to someone near you. Share what you wrote. That person is your accountability buddy. Check in with each other next week."

*Give 60 seconds to share.*

### 2:10–2:15 | CLOSE (5 min)

**Lead:** Co-facilitator B

**SLIDE:** Close

**Co-facilitator B says:**
> "Thank you all for bringing the energy today. Here's where to get help going forward:
> - The Teams channel is still active. Post questions anytime.
> - Next month's AI Forum will dig deeper into workflows you all care about.
> - Agueda, Co-facilitator A, and I are available if you want to brainstorm how Claude fits into your specific work."

> "Take what's useful from today. Leave what isn't. And remember: the skill isn't knowing the perfect prompt. It's being willing to iterate."

> "Thanks, everyone."

## Scoring Guide

### Point values by challenge

| # | Challenge | Points | Sprint |
|---|-----------|--------|--------|
| 1 | Find the Project's docs in Google Drive | 2 | 1 |
| 2 | Summarize a Project document | 3 | 1 |
| 3 | Extract milestones/deliverables | 3 | 1 |
| 4 | Compare two documents | 5 | 1 |
| 5 | Identify gaps/missing info | 5 | 1 |
| 6 | Synthesize across multiple docs | 7 | 1 |
| 7 | Create the Project 2.0 in Asana | 3 | 2 |
| 8 | Add sections/tasks framed around the new direction | 4 | 2 |
| 9 | Write a project status update | 3 | 2 |
| 10 | Organize tasks by priority/timeline | 5 | 2 |
| 11 | Capstone: takeaway slide | 7 | 2 |

**Sprint 1 total: 25 points · Sprint 2 total: 22 points · Grand total: 47 points**

### Scoring rules
- Honor system. Teams screenshot results as proof.
- Facilitators don't need to verify every challenge in real time.
- Partial credit at facilitator discretion (for example, attempted but Claude gave poor results = half credit if the prompt was good).
- Capstone (#11) must include BOTH the Project 2.0 vision AND the "one tedious real-work task" element to get full points.

## Judges' Table Facilitation Guide

**Purpose:** the Judges' Tables replace traditional reflection. They're framed as competition commentary: fast, energetic, surfacing patterns.

**Format:**
- **60 seconds per team** (Co-facilitator A keeps time)
- Two questions: "Best find?" and "Where did Claude fail you?"
- After all teams share, facilitators add 1-2 observations
- Total time: about 7 minutes per Judges' Table

**Facilitator observations to prepare**

Judges' Table #1 (after Sprint 1):
- Patterns in prompt iteration ("Most teams had to try 2-3 times")
- Where Claude excelled (finding docs, summarizing)
- Where Claude struggled (comparing docs, identifying gaps)
- Smart prompting strategies you noticed

Judges' Table #2 (after Sprint 2):
- How teams connected Google Drive research to Asana project planning
- Creative interpretations of the new direction
- Real-work pain points from capstone slides (collect these!)
- Best overall use of Claude as a coworker

## Backup Plans

**If Claude integrations aren't working for some participants**
- Pair them with someone whose setup works. Don't spend sprint time troubleshooting.
- Use the break for troubleshooting.
- Worst case: the participant can observe and help with prompting strategy.

**If teams finish Sprint 1 early**
- Push them toward Challenges #5 and #6 (harder, more points).
- Ask them to refine their earlier answers: "Can you get Claude to go deeper on #3?"

**If teams are behind in Sprint 2**
- Prioritize: "Focus on #7 and #8 first. Those build the foundation. Then jump to #11 for the capstone points."
- Challenge #10 (organize by priority) can be skipped if time is tight.

**If the new direction confuses people**
- That's fine. Say: "Ask Claude what it means and how it connects to the Project. That's literally the exercise."
- The point is using Claude to figure things out, not already knowing the strategy.

**If a team is disengaged**
- Visit them directly. Ask what they're stuck on.
- Give a concrete nudge: "Try asking Claude: 'What was the original goal of the Project?' and see what it finds."
- If one person is dominating, suggest: "Let someone else drive the next prompt."

**If timing is off**
- **Running long on Sprint 1:** cut to 35 minutes. Judges' Table #1 can go to 5 minutes.
- **Running long on Sprint 2:** cut the gallery walk to team-by-team callouts instead of walking around.
- **Running short:** extend "Your Turn," with more time to write and share.
