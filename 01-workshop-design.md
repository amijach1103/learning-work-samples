# Workshop design: Claude as a coworker

**Work sample, Agueda Schwartz.** The design document I wrote in January 2026 for a two-hour, hands-on workshop for the staff of a research and development lab. It sets the learning objectives, the flow, the design principles and how we'd know it worked. I updated it after the pilot and again after a staff forum session, and both sets of findings are in here. A week before the workshop the format changed to a team competition (see the facilitator track); this is the design it grew from. Names are removed, and the section-by-section facilitator script is left out for length.

---

## Foundation

### The problem
Everyone's figuring out AI alone. Some are ahead, some are stuck, nobody knows what others are doing. Meanwhile, the same busywork eats everyone's time: status updates, task tracking, first drafts.

### The direction
Claude is the AI tool we use. What's NOT decided is how you use it. This workshop is about finding what works for YOU within that direction.

### What's in it for you
- Reclaim time spent on tasks you resent
- Stay in flow instead of switching between apps
- Get a "first pass" so you can focus on the thinking, not the formatting

Whether you're already using AI daily or just getting started, this workshop meets you where you are.

### The mental model
**Claude is a coworker, not a tool.** You talk to it like a teammate: give it context, ask for help, review its work, give feedback.

It's not magic (it misses things), and it's not a replacement (you still decide). It's a good first pass that frees you up for the work that actually needs your brain.

### What we believe
- **This isn't a test.** The pilot participants struggled too, and that's normal.
- **You won't look dumb.** Specificity is a skill you build through iteration.
- **This isn't replacing you.** It's handling the stuff you hate doing.
- **It's okay to be skeptical.** We'll show you, not tell you.
- **There's no wrong way to start.** Iteration is the skill, not perfection.

### What to expect in this workshop
- You'll try things that don't work perfectly. That's the point.
- Ask questions anytime. There are no dumb ones.
- We'll be honest about what Claude does well AND where it falls short.

### What success looks like
- Everyone leaves having done one real workflow (not just watched)
- Everyone knows when to use Claude AND when not to
- Everyone knows where to get help when stuck
- People keep using it weekly after the workshop

### What we're NOT doing
- Teaching coding or Claude Code (terminal)
- Replacing anyone's judgment
- Prescribing exactly how to use it (autonomy matters)
- Promising it's faster (it's hands-off, not always faster)
- Leaving you on your own after

## What the pilot taught us

Five colleagues tested Claude with Asana and Google Drive on their own work for 2+ weeks. Here's what we learned:

| Theme | What they said |
|-------|---------------|
| **Integration value is real** | "I love the integration." One person set it up for personal use too. |
| **Good first pass, not final** | Extracted deliverables well, but needs human review |
| **Accuracy isn't 100%** | Missed some action items. Expect to review and edit. |
| **Specificity matters** | "Claude isn't a mind-reader." Precise prompts work better. |
| **Not always faster** | Simple Asana lookups are faster by hand, but Claude is hands-off |
| **Google Drive is read-only** | Can read docs, can't create or edit directly in Drive |

**Key insight:** frame Claude as "hands-off," not "faster."

Participants arrive with:
- [ ] Claude accessible (desktop app or browser)

- [ ] Account set up under the right work email

*Setup instructions sent in advance. Support available in the team chat if stuck.*

## Learning objectives

By the end of this workshop, participants will be able to:

1. **Understand the "Claude as coworker" concept:** AI assists, humans decide
2. **Identify friction in their own work:** "What do you hate doing that AI can handle?"
3. **Talk to Claude like a coworker:** ask for help using natural language
4. **Use Claude for common project management tasks:** status updates, task extraction, summaries
5. **Refine prompts** when Claude misunderstands: iterate, don't give up
6. **Review and edit Claude's output:** expect a good first pass, not a final product
7. **Recognize when NOT to use AI:** sensitive judgment, confidential info, or when doing it by hand is faster
8. **Understand integration limitations:** what Claude can and can't do with Google Drive and Asana
9. **Commit to one workflow** to try in the next week
10. **Know where to get help:** resources, who to ask

## Workshop flow (2 hours)

| Time | Duration | Section | Activity | Artifact |
|------|----------|---------|----------|----------|
| 0:00 | 5 min | **Welcome** | Context: why we're here, what to expect | |
| 0:05 | 10 min | **Ground in reality** | Write down one task you resented this week; quick share | Friction point identified |
| 0:15 | 5 min | **Show #1** | Demo: status update with Claude and Asana (live) | |
| 0:20 | 15 min | **Try #1** | Everyone tries the same prompt with their own Asana | |
| 0:35 | 5 min | **Troubleshoot** | "What didn't work?" Normalize imperfection. | |
| 0:40 | 10 min | **Make it yours #1** | Modify the prompt to fit how YOU report status | Personalized prompt saved |
| 0:50 | 5 min | **Show #2** | Demo: action items from a doc into Asana | |
| 0:55 | 15 min | **Try #2** | Everyone tries with their own doc or notes | |
| 1:10 | 10 min | **Peer check** | Pairs compare: what did Claude catch or miss? | |
| 1:20 | 10 min | **Reflection** | "Could Claude help with your friction task?" Pair discussion. | Connection to their pain |
| 1:30 | 10 min | **When NOT to use AI** | Group generates insights plus pilot learnings | |
| 1:40 | 15 min | **Commitment** | Write one workflow to try this week; share with a partner | Written commitment and buddy |
| 1:55 | 5 min | **Close** | Where to get help, next steps, thanks | |

## Design principles

| Principle | How it shows up |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Start with THEIR friction | Opening activity: "one task you resented" |
| Work on REAL tasks | They use their own Asana, their own docs |
| Produce an artifact | Leave with reusable prompts and a commitment |
| Show, try, make it yours | Brief demo, then practice, then personalize |
| Peer learning | Pairs throughout for troubleshooting and reflection |
| Normalize failure | "What did Claude miss?" built into the flow |
| Progressive complexity | Status update first (simpler), task extraction second |

---

## Artifacts participants leave with

1. Their identified friction point
2. A personalized status update prompt
3. A task extraction prompt (if time)
4. A written commitment for this week
5. An accountability buddy

## Before the workshop: setup and materials

### Room setup
- Projector or screen for demos
- Facilitators positioned to circulate easily
- Seating that allows pairs to form quickly

### Materials checklist
- [ ] Sample prompts ready to display or share in chat
- [ ] Backup prompts if participants don't have their own docs
- [ ] Resource handout or slide with post-workshop support info
- [ ] Paper or sticky notes for the friction point activity (optional)
- [ ] Commitment cards or template (optional)

### Facilitator prep
- [ ] Test the Claude and Asana demo on the projector beforehand
- [ ] Have a real project ready for the live demo (not fake data)
- [ ] Know the backup plan for participants without setup complete
- [ ] All three facilitators align on who leads which sections

### Tech backup plans
| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| Projector fails | Facilitator narrates while participants follow on their own screens |
| Claude is slow or down | Switch to discussion: "What would you ask Claude to do?" |
| Many people aren't set up | One facilitator runs setup support in a corner; continue with those who are ready |
| Wi-Fi issues | Pause, troubleshoot, adjust timing |

## Still refining

**Hands-on activities:**
- Status update workflow: is this the most relevant?
- Task extraction from docs: is this what people need?
- Are there other workflows that match their actual friction better?

**Open questions:**
- What are the 2-3 workflows that would be most universally useful?
- Should participants choose which workflow to try (a menu) or does everyone do the same one?
- Do we need pre-selected docs and tasks, or do they use their own live data?

## Staff forum input

At a staff AI forum session before the workshop, I showed how I use Claude in my own work and asked people what they wanted help with.

**Pain points identified by attendees:**

| Pain point | Detail | Workshop implication |
|------------|--------|---------------------|
| **Formatting loss** | Copy-pasting Claude's output into Google Docs loses bullets and tabs | Teach the markdown download workaround; acknowledge this limitation |
| **Integration barriers** | Letting Claude write directly to shared drives poses security risks | Reinforce that Google Drive is read-only; don't promise write access |
| **Manual overhead** | Copy-paste workflows create errors and require reformatting | Frame it as "hands-off mental load," not "seamless automation" |

**Approaches worth sharing:**
- **Instruction document:** one attendee wrote a detailed instruction doc for Claude defining roles, evidence requirements and constraints. Could be an advanced technique to mention.
- **Running context doc:** meetings are transcribed into a running doc that gives Claude context. An example of how to keep continuity.

**Focus areas requested:** project management automation, daily friction reduction, branding and formatting.

**Design consideration:** the formatting pain point is real. Consider adding a quick tip during the workshop: "If you need formatting preserved, use 'download as markdown' or ask Claude to format for plain text."

## Support before and after

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| Someone doesn't complete pre-work | Pair them with someone who did; troubleshoot during hands-on time |
| Integration issues on the day | A facilitator helps one-on-one while others continue |

|------|-----|
