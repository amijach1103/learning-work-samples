# AI Forum Presenter Kit: What AI Looks Like for Me

**Work sample, Agueda Schwartz.** A kit I wrote to recruit and brief colleagues presenting at a staff AI learning series, 2026. Names and internal links are removed.

**Context.** After the workshop, the lab's AI Forum, a professional development series run by the Ops team, became the place where staff keep learning from each other. I provide strategic support to the Forum, and wrote this kit to recruit and brief colleagues presenting a session on how they use AI. Each session saves real time for what isn't working yet, with the room working through the presenter's open questions.

## Step 1: Initial ask

Hi [name]. For the AI Forum on [date], I'm inviting colleagues to share a "here's what AI and I do together" look at their work. I'd love it if you could take about 10-15 minutes and cover:

- Here's what AI is doing well for me
- How I got AI to do these things well
- Where AI isn't working yet, or where I want it to take on more
- Anything I'd ask of the group that could apply to my work

## Step 2: Follow-up notes (send once they say yes; aim for 3+ weeks ahead)

Session: What AI Looks Like for Me, [date], [time] (virtual, with an in-person room option)

### What we're after

- Colleagues learning from colleagues: what's working for you and how you built it
- Real time spent on where it isn't working yet. No guarantee we solve everything, but it's worth a shot with this group in the room.

### What to cover

- Where AI is doing good work for you: what's actually landed
- How you pulled it off: the setup, files, prompts, and workflow people can copy
- Where you're still scratching your head: where it isn't working, or where you want it doing more
- What you'd ask of the group: anything we could share, recommend, or pair on

### Time and format

- The standard slot is about 20 minutes: 10-15 presenting, then 5-10 for the room to work through your open questions with you. Would you like more time? Just let me know and I can adjust the agenda.
- Questions throughout or at the end, your preference.
- Slides are optional; a screen share of the real thing usually works best. A 6-slide template is available if it helps you keep time.
- One thing worth protecting: leave real time for the last two points. They're the reason the session is built this way.

## Description template

**What AI Looks Like for Me: [Presenter name]** · AI Forum · [Day, date] · [time]

[Presenter name], [title, team], [optional: one credibility line, e.g., a past AI project]. They show how they've built AI into their day-to-day work, [one high-level line on what they've built].

They'll cover:

- Where AI is doing good work for them
- How they set it up: the tools, prompts, and workflow you can borrow
- Where it's still falling short, or where they want it to do more
- What they'd ask of the group

The second half is open time for the room to work through their open questions with them. Bring your own AI questions too.
